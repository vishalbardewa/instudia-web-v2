## The Lab vs. The Laptop

If you look at the enrollment numbers for Nagaland’s medical and nursing colleges this year, you’ll see something incredible. As of 2025-26, women make up nearly **69%** of students in medical and allied sciences across our state. Our sisters and daughters are leading the way in healthcare, and it’s a point of massive pride for our community.

But then, I look at my own sector—Software Engineering—and the picture changes.

According to the latest [Gender Statistics 2025](https://morungexpress.com/women-lead-in-medical-courses-in-nagaland-engineering-remains-male-dominated#:~:text=In%20proportional%20terms%2C%20women%20accounted,across%20the%20state's%20educational%20pipeline.) report, female enrollment in undergraduate engineering courses in Nagaland has actually dropped to just **17%**. While the rest of the world is talking about "Women in Tech," here in our hills, the engineering classroom still looks like a "boys' club." 

As the Director of **instudia**, I have to ask: *Why are our girls heading to the lab, but staying away from the IDE?*

---

## Why the Binary?

When I talk to parents in Dimapur or Kohima, there’s often a lingering belief that "coding" is a desk job for men who like to sit in the dark and drink coffee. There’s a fear that it’s too abstract, too "math-heavy," or perhaps not as "secure" as a government nursing job.

But in 2026, those old binaries are crumbling. Software engineering isn't just about math; it's about **problem-solving, creativity, and empathy.**

Actually, coding is very much like traditional Naga weaving. It requires a deep understanding of patterns, immense patience, and an eye for how small threads (or lines of code) come together to create a functional, beautiful whole. Our women have been "programmers" of culture for generations; now, it’s time to apply those same skills to the digital economy.

## The 2026 Advantage

The timing couldn’t be better. This year’s state budget introduced the **Chief Minister’s STEM Incentive Programme**, specifically aimed at getting more Naga youth—especially girls—into technical fields. On top of that, the new **Innovation Seed Fund** is looking to support 1,000 women entrepreneurs. 

The support is there. The technology is here. All that’s missing is the shift in mindset.

## Beyond "Male-Dominated"

At **instudia**, we are making a conscious effort to change the face of tech in Nagaland. We don't just teach Python or Frontend Development; we build environments where women feel they belong. 

We’ve seen that when a Naga girl learns to build a mobile app or manage a database, she doesn't just get a job—she gains a level of **financial independence** that is entirely mobile. Whether she's in a bosti in Mokokchung or a high-rise in Bangalore, her skills travel with her. She isn't tied to a specific location or a traditional "9-to-5" if she chooses to freelance.

---

## A Call to Our Sisters

To the young women of Nagaland: Don't let the 17% statistic scare you. Let it provoke you. 

Software engineering is the most powerful "equalizer" we have in 2026. Code doesn't care about your gender; it only cares if your logic is sound and your solution works. Whether you want to build an app for local farmers, create a platform for Naga artisans, or work for a global tech giant, the door is wide open.

At our center near Notun Bosti Gate, I see the change starting. Slowly but surely, the seats in our Python and Web Dev classes are being filled by determined women who are ready to "Break the Binary."

Nagaland needs your logic. We need your perspective. And most importantly, we need you at the keyboard.
