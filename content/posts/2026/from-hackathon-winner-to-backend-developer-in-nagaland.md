## Wait, I Won *What*?

Picture this: I'm Marzulu, fresh off a win at the National Institute of Technology Nagaland (NITN) Hackathon, and suddenly I've got a sponsored seat at **Instudia Nagaland**. No, I didn't fully believe it either.

My programming skills at the time? Let's just say they were *technically* skills — in the same way a baby deer technically knows how to walk. I knew enough to be curious, but the real world of backend development felt like a locked room I didn't have the key to. Spoiler: Instudia handed me the whole keychain.

## Day One: Everything Is Confusing and I Love It

Okay, "love" is generous. My first week was more like:

> *"What's an API? Why is this function not working? What do you mean I have a syntax error on line 47?!"*

Functions, loops, APIs — concepts I'd heard of but never truly wrestled with. I was especially fascinated (and terrified) by one question: *how do websites actually handle data?* Where does it go? Who's in charge back there?

I was about to find out.

## The Fundamentals: Building the Foundation

The course didn't just throw theory at me — it built things up brick by brick. I got hands-on with:

- **CLI tools & OOP** — learning to think in objects, not just instructions
- **Functions** — properly this time, not just copy-pasting from Stack Overflow
- **argparse & SQL** — making programs talk to the user *and* to databases

It sounds dry written out like that, but the moment you see data flow from a user's input all the way into a database and back out again? That's the moment programming clicks into something *real*.

## The Hard Parts (Yes, There Were Hard Parts)

Let me be honest: **Object-Oriented Programming tried to break me.**

Classes. Inheritance. Encapsulation. It felt like learning a new language inside a language. There were days I'd stare at an error message for an embarrassingly long time, convinced the computer was personally offended by my code.

Debugging was its own beast — that special kind of frustration where you *know* something is wrong, you just have absolutely no idea what, where, or why.

But here's the thing about struggling through hard problems with the right guidance: you come out the other side actually understanding it. Not just "I got it to work" — but *why* it works.

## Projects I Actually Built (And Survived)

This is where things got exciting. Real projects. Real code. Real GitHub commits.

- **[E-commerce CLI Tool](https://github.com/Marzulu-Maketh/python-ecom)** — a full command-line shopping experience, built from scratch
- **Strong Password Identifier** — because "password123" is a crime
- **CLI Rogue-like Game** — yes, I made a game. In the terminal. It's as cool as it sounds.

Each project wasn't just an exercise — it was proof that the concepts I'd been learning were actually *mine* to use.

## The Glow-Up Is Real

Looking back at where I started versus where I am now is genuinely wild. The growth hits different when you can measure it:

- Writing Python code that's actually clean and structured (future me will thank present me)
- Debugging without panicking — or at least, panicking *less*
- Understanding how to work with packages and SQL queries like they're tools, not mysteries
- Thinking in systems: how does this piece connect to that piece?

## Where I Stand Today

I can build things. Real, functional things.

Clean CLI programs. Structured backends. Tools that take input, process it, and deliver output — exactly as intended. That's not nothing. That's actually kind of everything when you started not knowing how a function worked.

## What's Next

I'm not done. Not even close. The roadmap ahead looks like:

- Building complex, scalable backend systems that can handle real-world load
- Diving into advanced frameworks and deployment pipelines
- Exploring authentication, security, and cloud integration — the stuff that makes applications *serious*

The foundation is solid. Now it's time to build higher.

## Final Thoughts

This journey took me from someone who was curious about backend development to someone who can actually *do* backend development. That shift — from observer to builder — is everything. For anyone just starting out, reading a [complete beginner's coding guide](/blog/how-to-learn-coding-guide-beginners) and following a clear [portfolio building strategy](/blog/how-to-build-your-portfolio-no-experience) makes all the difference.

Massive gratitude to **Instudia Nagaland** for making this possible. The guidance was practical, the projects were real, and the growth was undeniable.
